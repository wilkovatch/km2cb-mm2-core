## Race containers and opponents

Races remain custom city properties with `elementContainer` metadata, category `race`. Their stable `_elementContainerId` is referenced by `_propertyContainerId` on owned custom elements and mesh settings. Keep MM2 eligibility and contextual commands in core JSON. The opponent is a Low Poly-only spline, with `placementObject: carModel`: the first click positions the independent parametric car, later clicks append waypoints. Its `difficulty` is 0 for amateur and 1 for professional. Preserve the professional fallback to amateur when the professional set is empty.

Migration 5 moves legacy opponent arrays into owned spline elements through the ordinary backup mechanism. `race_elements.py` adapts authored controls to the existing AIMAP/OPP writer and writes race-scoped props. The `race` property on PTH parts carries the stable owner; `PATHSETWriter.write(output_path, race)` filters these separately from global props. `.km2-race-props.json` tracks only exporter-owned filenames for stale-file cleanup and is excluded from archives. Tests: `python exporters/psdl/tools/_test_races.py`, builder `KM2_SELFTEST_FOCUS=races`. Keep these checks short and synthetic.

Race difficulty editing uses nested property containers: `raceSetupAmateur` / `raceSetupProfessional` (and their Blitz variants) occupy the existing `policeCars` / `policeCars_professional` states. Their category is `raceDifficulty`, with parent category `race` and filtered inherited opponent membership (`difficulty` 0/1). Saved opponent owners remain the race identity; no exporter adapter or extra migration is needed. Do not reintroduce separate difficulty dropdowns for opponent or police creation. Shared checkpoints and props remain race-scoped. Focused builder coverage includes both difficulty selections, visibility, cancellation, presets, undo, clone and reload.

## Opponent route planning (midtown2.exe)

Reverse-engineered 2026-11-07 (setup in [exporting.md](exporting.md#reverse-engineering-setup)). The opponents **don't search for a route** between their waypoints:

- **OPP loader** (`aiVehicleOpponent` init 0x53d060, object size 0x9794; created in `AIMAP.Init` 0x5352e8): the header row is skipped, then every row's room is looked up (`lvlLevel` vtable +0x1c; none: `ERROR: Point - %d, Is not in a cull room.`). Row 0 is the start (x, y, z, and the 4th column, the yaw in degrees). The last row is the finish, kept as a position (+0x9774). Each middle row becomes the **first BAI intersection of its room** (component type 3 in the room list at 0x6b2fa0, stored as a short in the array at +0x9788); in any other room it logs `ERROR: Opp: %d, Point - %d, Is not a intersection.` and the slot stays **uninitialised** (a detour to a random intersection, or a NULL intersection). The count kept is rows - 2.
- **Route racer** (`aiVehiclePhysics`, init 0x5598a0, waypoints at +0x9674, count +0x9678, index +0x967a): `aiMap` 0x53a680 returns the road that is in the road lists of **both** intersections (or NULL). The racer keeps the roads `road(wp[i], wp[i+1])`, `road(wp[i+1], wp[i+2])`, `road(wp[i+2], wp[i+3])` (+0x26c/+0x270/+0x274). An index past the end gives `Returning a NULL Intersection` and a NULL road (harmless at the end of the list).
- **Driving states** (+0x27c, jump table 0x55aab4): 0 follows the current road, 1 the intersection, **2 drives straight** (entered at 0x55b5b0 when the current road is NULL; 0x55ab50 enter, 0x55abe0 update), 3 stopped. In state 2 the target (+0x9668) is the **centre of the next waypoint's intersection** (0x55c180, intersection +0x14); the waypoint is passed when the car's room lookup (0x537680) reports that intersection. Lane directions and road types play no part.
- **Passing a waypoint and turning** (2026-11-09): every frame of state 0 the car is located by 0x537aa0 (the room of its position, then in that room's component list the road hinted by `road(wp[i-1], wp[i])`, else the room's intersection, type 3, else its first road). The waypoint is passed (0x55c510) only while that lookup reports type 3 with the waypoint's intersection id; the index then moves on, the three roads are taken again and the path is planned along the new road (0x55d9f0). Its section is found by 0x5485e0: the first section whose plane (z axis, which points to the start of the road) the car hasn't passed, within `half width + 4 m` sideways of the centre, otherwise the sideways nearest; driving a road from its end this is `n - index`. State 1 (enter 0x55b400, update 0x55b460) steers at the first point of the planned path with steering `-2.857 * angle` (clamped to ±1) and throttle 0.85 until it faces it within 0.1 rad, or for 65 frames, then goes back to state 0 (state 2 without a road). So a car that has to turn back has its target behind it, inside its turning circle. Seen in game (2026-11-09, user, Desktop\testcity): a waypoint where the opponent turned by 153° from a PSDL road onto a BAI-only road, whose end was 45 m from the intersection centre and 129° from the car's direction (one-way sizing against a road meeting it at 27°), made the opponent circle there. Not verified separately: a sharp turn onto a road that starts at the intersection, and a U-turn. In 180 waypoints of 10 sampled stock OPP files (`random.Random(1)`) the sharpest road-to-road turn is 106° and the next road starts at most 68° from the arriving direction; there are no U-turns.
- **Circuits**: after the last waypoint, a remaining lap sets the index back to 1 (0x55c600): the car goes on to the second waypoint. The stock circuits close the loop: the first waypoint is the intersection behind the start (the start is on the road from it to the second waypoint) and the last waypoint is on that intersection again (9 of 10 sampled stock circuit opponents; the exception, sf `circuit11`, has no AIMAP or waypoints, an unused leftover). Seen in game (2026-11-08, a 5-lap test circuit, twice): when the last waypoint is not the first one's intersection and no road joins it to the second waypoint, the opponent circles around the last waypoint until the final lap, then drives to the finish. Not verified: a last waypoint joined to the second one by a road.
- `aiMap::CalcRoute` (0x53a7a0, Dijkstra over the intersections on whole road lengths, roads undirected; an unreachable destination reads an uninitialised index) is used by the police (0x53e4d8...) and the "Hookman" vehicles (0x54ace0...), **not** by the opponents.

Stock usage: the stock waypoints sit within ~12 m of intersection centres, starts and finishes on roads next to the first and last waypoint. About 10% of the consecutive stock waypoints are not adjacent: deliberate straight shortcuts (London race11 legs of 114 m and 55 m that are 494 m and 574 m by road). Some stock legs go straight through built blocks: sf `race7-p-3`, waypoints 4-5 and 7-8, cross the facade bounds of rooms 50 and 55 (blocks of ~130 m with 110 and 82 facades). Either the stock data is broken there or the game recovers stuck opponents; not verified in game yet.

**The check** (`exporters/psdl/traffic/opponents.py`, `OpponentMap.check`) replays this on the in-memory BAI network: the room of a position is `JsonProcessor.find_block(pos) + 1`, the same numbering as `BaiIntersection.room` (`RoomFinder`, in `traffic/rooms.py`, tests the block bounds with numpy first). Legs: `road` (a road joins the intersections; the start/finish legs when the position is in the waypoint's room or in a road room with an end on it), `straight` (warning: off the roads), `blocked` (error: a straight leg crossing a `_FACB` facade bound under its top, with the height interpolated between the ends, or points out of every room, sampled every 2 m, 3 m from the ends). On a circuit with more than one lap whose last waypoint isn't on the first one's intersection there is also the leg from the last waypoint back to the second one (`Leg.lap`, drawn, but reported only as the error `opponent_circuit_open`, which explains how to close the loop). At each waypoint between two road legs (and on a closed circuit at the last one, into the leg from the first waypoint to the second), `waypoint_turn` measures the turn (arriving vs leaving direction of the centre lines) and the angle from the arriving direction to the start of the next road: over `TURN_MAX` (135°) or `AIM_MAX` (90°, behind the car) it is the error `opponent_turn_too_sharp`. Turns next to straight legs are not measured (they end at the intersection centre, and stock sf `race7-p-3` and london `race2-p-3` have sharp ones there). `race_elements.opponent_laps` gives the laps of the opponent's difficulty (`laps`, `laps_professional`; the amateur opponents drive the professional laps too when the professional set is empty, as in the export). `race_elements.check_opponents` reports the issues with the leg as the overlay (green road, orange straight, red blocked). It runs in the `checks/validateOpponents` check (errors, plus an `opponent_route` info per opponent with its whole route) and in `CinfoAimapExporter.export_cinfo_aimap` (as warnings, which don't fail the export). `JsonProcessor.get_objects(traffic_only=True)` stops after the network (MM3 Paris: 0.1 s, the whole check 0.5 s, about 1.1 s through the builder with the export JSON).

**The editor enforces the waypoints on intersections**: the opponent type has `intersectionTargets` and `acceptTarget: opponents.acceptTarget` (`lua/opponents.lua`). Every waypoint but the last one goes only on the center of an intersection, which it follows (`IntersectionAnchor` in the builder, saved as `linkType: Centre`). Seamless intersections are refused: their roads can be a single BAI road chain, and a waypoint there is never needed. While adding waypoints only the intersections are shown (the builder shows the targets of a point followed by another one), and `targetsHint: OPPONENT_WAYPOINTS_HINT` explains the rule in the refusal errors and in a `label:` of the opponent panel. A click on another opponent's waypoint goes on its intersection (intersection-anchored points are never shared). The finish (`ctx.index == ctx.count`) goes anywhere. A point can't be added after a free finish (the builder refuses changes that leave an accepted point where it isn't accepted any more): the user adds waypoints before it (**Add before selected**), or puts the finish on an intersection first. While points are added, a click on a waypoint of the same opponent adds another waypoint on its intersection (the builder's `CustomElementPlacer.AddsOnIntersection`, consuming the click so the gizmo doesn't select the point), which is how a circuit is closed; a waypoint next to one on the same intersection is refused (`CustomElements.NextToSameCentre`, also in `customElement.insert`). The panel lists the waypoints (`spline.pointsList`, the last one labelled the finish) with the selected one highlighted. A waypoint whose intersection is deleted stays free where it was, and the check reports it. There is no migration: the free waypoints of existing cities stay as they are (the check reports them, and **Attach** in the point panel puts one on an intersection). The check still guards everything else (chains, rooms, straight legs).

Tests: `python exporters/psdl/tools/_test_opponent_routes.py` (synthetic legs and turns; with `KM2_MM2_STOCK_CITY` a fixed sample of 6 stock OPP files per city against the stock PSDL rooms, facade bounds and BAI: no structural errors, blocked legs listed with `KM2_VERBOSE=1`), builder `--focus races` (`OpponentRouteCheck`: API, button, panel, overlay; `OpponentIntersectionWaypoints`: API targets, insertion, reversing, attaching, moved and deleted intersections, undo, clicks (a real mouse click on a waypoint of the opponent), the waypoint list, reload). The race tests make their intersections with BAI-only roads (`WaypointIntersections`), which have no geometry over the opponent lines, and delete them before the whole-race checks (their "not over any room" warnings would be in the results).

## Cops & Robbers placement (midtown2.exe)

Reverse-engineered 2026-11-09 from `midtown2_orig.exe` (CnR init 0x423c80, placement each round 0x424ad0). The objects are the gold `wpobj_gold` (+0xb208), base A `pt_hideout` (+0xb200) and base B `pt_bank` (+0xb204); CnR mode 2 (ds:0x6b18bc) uses `pt_red`/`pt_blue` as the bases. Two files in `race/<city>/` drive the placement:

- **`multicopsets.csv`** (loader 0x424640, `%srace\%s\%ssets.csv`; neither stock nor any addon city we know ships it): rows in groups of 3, each group a fixed set. Row 1 is the bank, row 2 the gold, row 3 the hideout. Each round picks a random set other than the previous one. Game bug: the bank takes row 1's x and y but row 3's z. Not exported, not tested in game.
- **`multicopwaypoints.csv`** (loader 0x424880, read only without sets, ignored with fewer than 3 rows): stock header and rows `x,y,z,heading,0,0,0,0,`; only x, y and z are read. Each of the three objects independently (0x424cd0): 50% a random row, `rand % (count - 1)` so **the last row is never used**, 50% a random road position from 0x413b70 (the cruise spawn routine, see the freeway hang in [exporting.md](exporting.md)), lowered by 2 m. Rows are used as they are. The three positions are re-rolled until distinct. Stock London has 46 rows, SF 44.

The editor side is the `cnrSpots` city property (**Cops & Robbers** tab), a container like `roamPoliceCars` (category `race`, Create entry `CREATE_CNR_SPOT`) holding `cnrSpot` object instances. Their placement type is `elements/props/parametric/cnrSpot.json`: every `objectInstanceSettings.type` must have a file there, or the builder throws `KeyNotFoundException` in `MeshInstance.GetPlacementType`. `CinfoAimapExporter.export_cnr_spots` writes them with the X mirrored and repeats the last spot so that every spot can be picked and there are at least 3 rows; with no spots it deletes a stale file, and it warns about spots outside every room (`RoomFinder`). Not verified in game yet: the height the gold expects for a row (whether the row y needs an offset, as the road positions get -2 m). Tests: `python exporters/psdl/tools/_test_races.py` (`CnrSpots`), builder `--focus races` (`ContainerMenu`).

## Independent point values

Builder program version 5, feature version 1 saves non-anchorable leaves inline under `points`, with `position` and enabled `rotation`/`scale` vectors. `parkedCar`, `decalSingle` and `mapLabel` explicitly set `point.anchorable: false`; they have no shared point registry id and no M control-point editor. Migration 6 converts their saved centers before decoding, copies the saved world transform, preserves the original shared-point ids for other references, validates before writing, backs up the city/preferences and publishes coreVersion 6 last. The builder has no fallback for earlier referenced centers. Checks: `python exporters/psdl/tools/_test_independent_points_migration.py`, builder `KM2_SELFTEST_FOCUS=independentPoints`. The Lua geometry context and exporter JSON point shape stay the same.

## Instance state of the splines

The values of a road's spline (`project`, `projectAll`, `curveType`, `segments`, `segmentsPer100m`, `lpf`, `adjustLowPolyWidth`, `hermiteTension`, `subdivideEqually`), `makeCoplanar` and `warp` on roads (`warp` also on intersections), and `projectToGround` on building lines are in the instance state, so presets don't carry or change them. The initial values are in `elements/roads/*/settings.json` and `elements/buildings/psdl/line_settings.json`, and match what the templates used to set. `warp` and `makeCoplanar` on roads have `reuseInNewRoads`, so a road drawn in the editor takes them from the last road selected, with the spline values. `makeCoplanar` is a parameter of the `psdl` and `dummy` road types (`defaultValue` true, the initial value of the roads made with the API too), applied by `lua/coplanar.lua`, the `postProcess` of those types; its tolerance is `pointPlaneTreshold`, a hidden parameter in the presets (0.02 when missing). The keys are the ones the builder used when it did this itself, so the cities need no migration. Terrain patches keep `warp` and `projectToGround` in their state, because they have no presets. Spline custom elements keep these values in their single state, excluded from presets, and the decal's Bézier default moved from its preset to `spline.defaultCurveType`.

Migration 5 moves the values of the existing elements and removes them from the presets, only under the listed spline types for `customElement/<type>` (another type can have its own `segments`). A key that is missing stays missing, so it reads as before. Equivalence check: export an old city with the old build, migrate a copy, export again and compare PSDL, BAI and INST (0 differences expected). Checks: `python exporters/psdl/tools/_test_spline_state_migration.py`, builder `KM2_SELFTEST_FOCUS=splineState`.
