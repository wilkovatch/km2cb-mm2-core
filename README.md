This core for km2 City Builder adds support for Midtown Madness 2 cities

The main repository for the city builder is here: https://github.com/wilkovatch/km2-city-builder

The exporter is included directly in this repository, including `exporters/psdl/common` and its original license. Cloning the core does not require submodules.

## Creating races

Create and select a race in City properties. **Create > Checkpoint** adds directly to that race. Open its **Amateur** or **Professional** subcontainer to edit that difficulty's police cars and opponents: **Create > Police car** and **Create > Opponent** use the selected difficulty automatically. Only that setup's police cars and opponent routes are visible and selectable. Blitz races have police cars but no opponents. While the police cars of the roam mode are being edited, **Create > Police car** adds to those instead.

The race panel shows each setting on one row, with the amateur value on the left and the professional one on the right. Its Amateur and Professional buttons open the difficulty setups; each has separate Police cars and Opponents lists with Edit and Delete. Checkpoints and props stay at race level. The blue container menu shows **2 containers selected** for a race and its difficulty, with entries to reopen or deselect each. Deselecting only the difficulty keeps race props and checkpoints visible; switching or deselecting the race clears the difficulty too.

For an opponent, the first click places its starting car, and later clicks draw the waypoint route as a Low Poly spline. The car's position and heading remain independently editable. Its panel edits the car model and tuning; difficulty comes from the selected subcontainer. An empty professional set still uses the amateur opponents when exported. **Back** returns to the list the element was opened from, or to its difficulty setup if an opponent was clicked in the city (the race for props). Deleting a checkpoint or police car clicked in the city just closes its panel; deleting one opened from a list returns there.

The **Props** submenu also turns blue: individual props, mass placement, prop lines and polygons created there belong to the selected race. Deselect the race with the blue container menu to place ordinary city props. Race props appear only while their race is selected; radius erasing affects only props in that scope. Changing races cancels placement into the old race.

The race panel's Contents list edits and deletes owned elements. Cloning a race copies its contents with independent control points; deleting a race removes them together, with undo/redo support. Race props export separately to `race/<city>/<race-id>.pathset`, alongside that race's AIMAP and opponent files, and are included in archive exports. The exporter tracks its own race prop filenames to remove stale files after race deletion or reordering.

Core migration 5 backs up older cities and converts their existing opponents to owned spline elements, preserving starting cars, tuning, difficulty, waypoint order and incomplete routes. Race settings, checkpoints and police cars remain in their existing containers.

Difficulty subcontainers use the existing saved opponent difficulty and police arrays, so existing cities keep their data without an additional migration. Cloning, deleting, undoing and reloading a race preserve both setups. Export formats and the professional fallback are unchanged.

## Surface projection

The terrain grid is a placement guide in MM2. Elements that automatically project onto city surfaces, such as decals, first search roads, intersections, terrain patches, supported custom geometry and INST meshes. They use the grid only if no supported city surface is found, even when that surface is several metres below it. Roads and other elements that explicitly follow the heightmap keep doing so.

The core enables `surfaceProjectionTerrainFallbackOnly` in its settings and `meshInstance.projectionSurface` for the INST mesh type. Props are not additional projection targets. Projected spline sections follow edits to INST meshes, including transforms, replacement, deletion and undo/redo; the saved control points retain their placement and move rules.

## Importing the stock game assets

Open or create a city using the Midtown Madness 2 core, then choose **Scripts → Import MM2 game assets...**. Select the folder of your own MM2 installation (the folder containing `MM2CORE.AR` and `MM2TEX.AR`) and click **Run**. The script extracts the stock meshes and their MTX files, TEX and TGA textures, prop tuning (`tune/banger/*.dgBangerData`) and `city/materials.csv` to the city builder's `Files/common/MidtownMadness2` folder. The city templates already search this folder for shared assets.

Existing files are skipped by default, including names with different capitalization. Enable **Overwrite existing files** to replace matching assets with the stock versions. Unrelated files and the placeholder files are preserved. The importer reads only the two stock DAVE archives: mods, loose overrides, audio, collision bounds, city/race files and JPG images (including the optional map frame) are not imported. No extracted game data is included with the core.

The progress bar has a **Cancel** button. Completed files remain after cancellation or an error, and importing cannot be undone. Rerun with overwrite disabled to fill in the missing files. Each completed file is published only after its data has been validated; forcibly stopping the script can leave a `.km2-mm2-import-*.tmp` file, which is harmless and can be deleted. When the import finishes, it reports the destination, imported/replaced/skipped counts and bytes written. **Save and reopen the city** to refresh cached meshes, textures and prop transforms.

The SF and London city templates include building presets based on the facades of the original cities: 69 named styles for SF and 62 for London, in addition to their Default presets. They include matching building-side and building-block presets, using the original texture combinations, facade tile sizes and roof textures. Choose them in the building editor or in Whole city buildings. The stock textures must be provided in `Files/common/MidtownMadness2/texture` as usual. These are reusable procedural styles: each repeats its facade on all sides, and ground-floor slivers are adapted to editable texture blocks. Slope infill uses matching plain masonry, plaster or siding instead of repeating ground-floor doors and windows. The source rooms, dimensions and textures are listed in [Building presets](BUILDING_PRESETS.md). Existing cities keep their own presets; the template presets are copied when creating a new city.

The builder exports directly to the game formats. BIN-only export remains available as an intermediate format for inspection and tools; Blender integration is no longer maintained.

Exporters supported (accessible by selecting the PSDL format when exporting):
 - PSDL
 - BAI
 - INST
 - PATHSET (props and decals)
 - Prop rules
 - PKG and bounds (BND, BBND, TER) of the elements exported to PKG
 - CINFO and AIMAP (races, opponents, police, ambient traffic)
 - Hudmap (the map of the HUD) and race maps (the maps of the race menus)
 - BIN (select "BIN only" to save the intermediate exporter data)

The files are written as in the folder of the game, ready to be copied there (or packed in an .ar file). The PSDL goes in a `city` folder: if the chosen .psdl file is in a folder named `city`, it is used as it is, otherwise a `city` folder is created in the chosen folder. The other folders are next to the `city` folder:
```
city/<city>.psdl, .inst, .bai, .aimap
city/<city>/        props.pathset, decals.pathset, proprules.csv, propdefs.csv
tune/               <city>.cinfo, <city>.mmHudMap
race/<city>/        roam.aimap, the race aimaps, opponents and waypoints, mm*data.csv, <city>_train.pathset, <city>_sailboat.pathset, <city>_ferry.pathset, <city>_parkedcar[_<race>].pathset
geometry/           <city>_mesh<n>.pkg, hudmap_<city>.pkg
bound/              <city>_mesh<n>_BOUND.bnd/.bbnd/.ter
texture/            <city>_hudmap<n>.tex, <city>_hudmap<n>_ni.tex
jpg/                <city>_maproam.jpg, <city>_map<race>.jpg
<city>_hudmap_preview.png (a preview of the hudmap, not used by the game)
<city>_hudmap_labels_preview.png (the same map with the roam labels, not used by the game)
```

To get a single file instead, choose the "MM2 archive (.ar)" file type when exporting (the "Pack to .ar archive" flag is then set). The same files are written to a temporary folder and packed, with the same layout, in `<city>.ar` (a zip archive, which the game reads like its own archives), together with the `geometry` and `texture` folders of the city (the custom meshes and textures). Exported files override custom files with the same name. The `.ar` and both HUD map preview PNGs are written next to the chosen file. Put the `.ar` in the folder of the game.

Additional features:
- Railings
  - You can place railings on roads and terrain patches.
  - For roads, go to the Railing tab and just set the railing properties.  
The Continue checkboxes at the bottom of the tab control how they are placed on neighboring intersections.
  - For terrain patches, go to the Railing tab, click Add railing, set the properties, and select the perimeter points over which the railing has to be placed.  
  To do so: click Switch to railing placement, then click on the terrain patch perimeter points.  
  Make sure the perimeter points will be the actual block perimeter points, otherwise it will not work properly ingame. (as railings are placed on the perimeter points of the block, not of the terrain patch)

- AI paths (BAI)
  - Ambient vehicles follow the traffic lanes shown in the editor, so the lane spaces, the parking spaces and different numbers of lanes per side are kept ingame. The BAI fields are computed as in the stock San Francisco and London files (route points 15 m from each end, lane boundaries, sidewalk distances, flags, etc.).
  - At most 4 lanes per side are supported by MM2, extra lanes are dropped with a warning in the export log.
  - The road flags come from the Traffic tab of the road: Divided road, Pedestrians only (alley) and Freeway (ambient vehicles go faster). Level roads get the flat flag automatically.
  - `exporters/psdl/tools/bai_check.py` checks a BAI file (stock or exported) against the stock conventions: `python bai_check.py --lint <file.bai>` reports problems, `--derive` recomputes the derived fields from the paths of the file and reports the maximum error.
- Cable cars
  - Check Cable car line in the Traffic tab of the roads of the line: they get a rail in each direction, shown in the editor as orange traffic lanes. As in San Francisco, the rails are 2.5 m from the center of the road by default (Cable car rails dist.).
  - Connect the roads of a line through intersections. The game puts one cable car (va_cablecar_f) at each end of a line, meaning each intersection where exactly one cable car road arrives, like the 4 ends of the San Francisco lines. A line that is only a closed loop has no ends, so it gets no cable cars: leave it open, or add a short cable car road from the loop to a dead end. The export log warns about lines without ends.
  - To join visible tram rails across an intersection, use **Generate rail decals** in its panel. The button is enabled with at least two connected tram roads. It pairs the most opposite roads first, with the existing turn-angle limit, and creates editable decal elements only across the intersection. They use `r4i_rails_f`, 2 m wide; edit the generated decals to change either. Road-length decals are placed separately. Generation appends a new set each time: delete old decals manually before regenerating.
- Low LOD textures
  - The game draws a road with one texture at the lowest level of detail. It shows the sidewalks, the road and the divider in proportion to their widths, so a stock texture such as `r6_lo_f` only looks right on roads of about the same widths. **Generate low LOD texture**, below the textures in the Main tab of a road, makes the one for the road: it reads its road, sidewalk and divider textures and widths, writes `texture/<road texture>_lod_<hash>.tex` in the city folder and sets it as the Low LOD texture of the road. Generate it again after changing them: the texture isn't updated by itself.
  - The button is enabled on the Double roads with a road texture, as the other roads have no low LOD texture in the PSDL. Roads with the same result share one file (each new one counts towards the limit of textures of a city). Undo restores the previous texture of the road, but the file stays in the folder, and is packed in the `.ar` with the rest of the folder: delete the ones no longer used by hand. When the road texture has a `_fa` version, as the stock ones do, a `_fa` version of the low LOD texture is written too.
  - With "MM2 archive (.ar)" the texture is exported with the city. Otherwise copy it, as the other custom textures, from the `texture` folder of the city to the one of the game.
- Export to PKG
  - Check Export to PKG on a road, intersection, terrain patch or building line to export it as a mesh instead of PSDL geometry. It saves PSDL vertices and allows shapes that the PSDL can't have.
  - Roads, intersections and terrain patches still become blocks, but empty ones (with the perimeter of the element and no surface), and the element is placed in its block through the INST file. Building lines are placed in the block they are on, as usual.
  - The meshes are written to `geometry/<city>_mesh<n>.pkg` (low LOD only, used by the game at every distance) and their colliders to `bound/<city>_mesh<n>_BOUND.bnd/.bbnd/.ter`. The meshes are relative to the bottom center of their bounding box.
  - The materials of the colliders (the ground type, e.g. cobblestone or grass) come from the textures of the polygons, as in the PSDL, through the `city/materials.csv` file of the game: copy it to `Files/common/MidtownMadness2/city/materials.csv` (next to the `geometry` and `texture` folders of the game files), and add the custom textures to both copies. Without it, or for textures not in it, the colliders use the `default` material.
  - Uncheck Export PKG and bound files in the export dialog to write only the INST entries and the empty blocks (e.g. when the meshes didn't change). A collider can have at most 65535 vertices and polygons; the TER file at most 65535 polygon references (about 10 m cells), bigger ones are skipped with a warning.
- Boats, ducks and ferries
  - Choose **Create > Moving objects > Other moving object** and draw the route of one boat with the usual spline point tools. Each route is an independent custom element and a **closed loop**: the boat moves along it for ever and the last point connects back to the first (click the first point to finish). The route needs at least three points.
  - **Model** is one of the stock models of the game: sailboat (the default model of the sailboat path file), sailboard, duck, tug, water taxi or car ferry. The game must have the model (the stock ones do). **Speed** is in metres per second (each boat varies by 1 m/s in game); 0 uses the default of the model (2 m/s for ducks, 20 for sailboards, 5 for the others). The car ferry always moves at the same speed and ignores it.
  - The cyan line previews MM2's own interpolation: a cubic curve through the points on X, Y and Z (with the neighbours of the first and last points wrapped), so the height of the points is the height of the boat (the stock boats are at -2 m, the water level). There is no generated model and points keep their authored heights, without surface projection.
  - The routes are exported directly to `race/<city>/<city>_sailboat.pathset` (boats, ducks, tugs, water taxis) and `<city>_ferry.pathset` (the car ferry), as in the stock cities. Re-exporting with no valid route removes the old file. Routes of the race variants (`<city>_sailboat_<race>.pathset`) and the bridges are not supported, and the routes of existing cities are not imported.
- Trains
  - Choose **Create > Moving objects > Train** and draw a route with the usual spline point tools. Each train is an independent custom element using London's train-path behavior. The model is hardcoded by the game; train paths have no presets. Existing city-property train lines migrate automatically with a backup.
  - The always-visible yellow line previews MM2's own interpolation from the second control point to the penultimate one, where the train stops in game. The outermost points still shape the curve. There is no cyan control polygon. The displayed route is lifted 0.25 m in world Y to keep it visible above slightly uneven ground; its selection collider follows the lift, with normal surface occlusion. There is no generated train, track or tunnel mesh, and points keep their authored heights without surface projection. The lift only affects the editor preview, not the exported train path.
  - MM2 uses cubic Hermite X/Z with fixed half-difference tangents and wrapped endpoint neighbors, but linear height between each pair of points. This train-only curve has no editable tension. The preview is static: it does not show movement or reversal timing. At least three points are needed for export; routes with fewer than four points remain editable but have no yellow route line.
  - While drawing trains or decals, the placement reference is yellow: the last point for append, the first for prepend, or the selected point for insertion before/after it. Turning placement off restores normal point colors.
  - The authored control points are exported directly to `race/<city>/<city>_train.pathset`, as in London (type 2, spacing byte 20). Display tessellation does not change export points. Re-exporting with no valid routes removes an old train pathset at that output location.
  - The tunnels and stations are not generated: place them as mesh instances, for example with the stock London meshes (`bl_subway*`, `cl_subway*`, `kl_subway*`, `ol_subway*`, `wl_subway*`) on invisible terrain patches (empty blocks), and put the points of the line inside them (the London trains run about 13 m below the streets).

- Decals
  - The decals are in **Create > Decals**, as **Spline** (**Decal (spline)**, below) and **Single** (**Decal (single)**). A single decal is a textured quad placed with one click and edited like a mesh instance: the gizmo is on the decal itself (**Move**, **Rotate** and **Scale**, or W, E, R) and its panel has the same transform fields. It moves on X and Z only (its height follows the roads and the ground, as the points of a spline decal), turns around Y only, and its scale X is the width and its scale Z the length. The texture is tiled as a spline decal does it: square tiles as wide as the quad, a whole number along it. It is exported as one piece of `decals.pathset`, so keep it under 25 m, on the blocks, and facing up (a mirrored one, with a negative scale, would be culled). Only the center of a single decal is put on the surface: on a slope the quad stays flat. **Project to ground** is hidden because the center already follows the surface. Presets include the size (scale), which can also be edited before placement and in preset management; position and rotation stay with each decal. Older presets without a size use the default 2 m width and 10 m length.
  - The properties panel of a spline decal opens immediately with **Create > Decals > Spline**, so texture, width and curve mode can be chosen before placing any points. Decals support the normal preset controls and preset management, including syncing linked decals while keeping their points and attachments. The last selected or saved preset is reused for new decals during the current city session; unsaved edits are not carried over.
  - The Default preset uses the SF tram-rail texture `texture/r4i_rails_f.tex`, 2 m wide, and new decals placed by hand use a Bézier spline (the spline type is not part of the presets). Existing cities receive this Default only if their decal preset category is missing. The intersection rail generator continues to produce low-poly decals independently of the selected preset.
  - Textured strips lying on roads or the ground, like the London zigzags (`decal_zigzag_l`), yellow boxes (`decal_x_inter_l`) and San Francisco rails (`r4i_rails_f`). Choose **Create > Decal** and place its individual points as for roads. Select its texture, width and curve mode (low-poly, Bézier or Hermite); insert, remove or move points in its editor. Points project onto the surface when placed or moved. The strip remains visible when its point handles are hidden.
  - They are exported with the PATHSET, to `city/<city>/decals.pathset` (next to `props.pathset`), as the game reads it: each decal is a strip of the texture tiled along it in square tiles as wide as the strip, with a whole number of tiles in each piece. The texture must be in the game (it's not exported), its transparent parts show what's under the decal. Texture names can have at most 31 characters.
  - The game draws each piece of a decal (every segment, split every 25 m at most) only when the room (block) where it starts is visible: keep decals on the blocks, the export log warns about points outside of them.
  - `exporters/psdl/tools/decal_check.py` checks a decals.pathset file (stock or exported): the faces, the tiles and the names.

- Hudmap and race maps
  - Exported with Export hudmap and race maps (it needs the Python module Pillow, installed with the requirements of the core). The map is drawn from the geometry of the city, seen from above with the north (-z in the game) up, with the colors set in the Hudmap tab of the city properties (by default the ones of the stock San Francisco map: white roads with a grey outline, beige ground and buildings, green grass, red landmarks, blue water).
  - The hudmap is the map of the HUD in the game: tiles of 256 pixels (or 512) at 5.5 m per pixel (as the stock maps) in `texture/<city>_hudmap<n>.tex` (with a `_ni` copy for the night), placed in the city by `geometry/hudmap_<city>.pkg`, and `tune/<city>.mmHudMap` (the size and zoom of the map, as in San Francisco, and the color around it, the water color). Tiles with only water are left out. Keep the map within 16 tiles (change the meters per pixel or the tile size otherwise). `<city>_hudmap_preview.png` shows the whole map.
  - The race maps are the images of the race menus (242x184, with the frame of the menu): `jpg/<city>_maproam.jpg` (the whole city) and `jpg/<city>_map<race>.jpg` for each race (blitz0, circuit0, race0, ...: the area of its checkpoints, the start in red and the checkpoints in green). The frame is a plain color, unless a stock map image is copied to `Files/common/MidtownMadness2/jpg/mapframe.jpg`: its frame is used instead.
  - Map labels (district names, etc.) are added with **Create > Map label** while the Hudmap tab of the city properties is open. The Map labels button lists the labels owned by this section. Each has text, a size (in pixels of the race maps), an angle and a position, placed and moved like a single decal. They are drawn on the menu maps. Export also writes `<city>_hudmap_labels_preview.png`, the full HUD preview with the labels enabled for the roam map (All or Roam only). The original `<city>_hudmap_preview.png` and the in-game HUD textures have no labels. Both preview PNGs are written next to an exported `.ar`, outside the archive.
  - Opening the Hudmap tab, its contents list or a label editor selects the Hudmap container, and all labels appear in the city. The core generates smooth filled outlines from the bundled Liberation Sans Bold font, using Meters per pixel (5.5 by default) for scale. The text is raised 5 m above its authored position, white with a black outline and no shadow, since it is an aid for placing the labels and not a preview of the final map (the Labels colour only affects the exported maps); the centre marker and saved/exported position stay on the ground, which the position follows when the city is loaded or the surface under it changes. Latin accents and Unicode uppercase are preserved. Text, angle, size and position edits update the geometry. The labels stay visible and can be clicked after closing the panels, until the Hudmap container is deselected from the Containers menu or the city properties switch from the Hudmap tab to another one. Older cities migrate their existing labels automatically with a backup.
  - Exported names use a small uppercase bold sans-serif style with spaced letters, in translucent black so that the map shows through as in the original SF and London maps, and the race start and checkpoints are drawn over them. Labels are 6.75 pixels on race maps by default, and roam-map labels are 75% of the race-map size. The default is the same bundled font used by the editor; an explicit Label font override affects exported maps. The original typeface is not identified by the baked stock JPEGs, so the bundled face is a compatible substitute. See [font source and license](fonts/README.md).
  - Roads and intersections are drawn as roads, terrain patches as ground; both are grass or water instead by the physics material of their texture in `city/materials.csv` (grass, mud, water, deepwater) or by its name (grass, park, lawn, water, pond, river, sea); perimeter-only patches are not drawn. The roofs of the building lines are drawn as buildings, mesh instances are not drawn. Change it with the Hudmap category of roads, intersections, terrain patches, building lines, buildings and mesh instances (e.g. Landmark for the buildings in red, Ground for a walkway inside a block, or Hidden); a mesh instance is drawn as its bounding box. The parts of the roads passing through an intersection take the category of the intersection unless they have their own.

Some things to consider when creating cities:
- Changing of mesh instances scale in unsupported in MM2
- Mesh instances have two types, chosen when they are created (**Create → Individual prop** or **Create → INST mesh**) and not interchangeable: individual props go in the PATHSET file (as props, on the props layer, with mass placement and erasing in a radius), INST meshes in the INST file (in the block under them, with the INST modifiers flags). Cities saved before the types get them from their former "Place as prop" flag when they are migrated.
- The game draws a prop (as the individual props, the props of the prop rules and the traffic lights) with its mesh moved by its centre of gravity, the `CG` of `tune/banger/<prop>.dgBangerData`, and the editor does the same: copy the `tune/banger` folder of the game to `Files/common/MidtownMadness2/tune/banger` (next to the `geometry` and `texture` folders of the game files). The editor reads it when the city is loaded. Without it the props are put with their lowest point on the ground (a warning in the log says so), which is not always where the game draws them (e.g. the fences, drawn from one end). A prop without banger data (e.g. a custom prop) is also put with its lowest point on the ground.
- To make MM2-style buildings with no real ground floors, set the ground floor height to 0, this way only the top block will be used to make the sliver.
- Invisible terrain patches will generate empty blocks in MM2, which can contain other things. (INST buildings, PSDL buildings, etc.)
- In MM2 only horizontal scaling is supported for INST meshes visible from all angles. Furthermore, the collider will not be scaled if present.
- Road sections in MM2 must be coplanar, the MM2 template has the option to force this set. It only affects roads that bend both horizontally and vertically, but the result can be ugly for non-slight curves.
- Roads, intersections and terrain patches will become blocks in the PSDL file, make sure that they are interconnected vertex by vertex. Also make sure that each building line only stays on one border between two blocks. This is mainly an issue with the automatically generated outer ring of buildings in the city.
- `exporters/psdl/tools/psdl_check.py` checks the block perimeters of a PSDL file (stock or exported): `python psdl_check.py --verbose <file.psdl>` lists the edges inside the city that lead to no block (thin spikes and slits of a perimeter). In the game the cars that cross them leave the city ("PHYS.DeclareMover : mover not even in city" in mm2.log). The exporter removes the spikes thinner than 10 cm, e.g. at the sidewalk corners of intersections where two roads meet at a right angle. It also checks that the blocks with PSDL props only use textures with an index below 255 (see below).
- MM2 places the PSDL props (the prop rules) only along blocks whose textures have an index below 255 in the PSDL texture list, otherwise it quits while loading the city ("Invalid command"), or crashes with MM2Hook (mm2.log: exception at EIP=0045da58 after "Placing props along road segments"). The exporter gives the lowest indices to the textures of the divided roads and then to those of the blocks with props. If they still don't fit (more than about 255 textures between them), the export log shows a warning and the props of those blocks are not exported.
- MM2 can load only a limited number of different objects (the meshes of the INST file, the props and the vehicles share about 4096 slots, and each different object in the INST file takes 6 of them), past it it crashes while loading the city (mm2.log: exception at EIP=004a48d8 after "Loading objects...", followed by "memMemoryAllocator::FindHeap failed"). Instances of the same mesh count once, but each element exported to PKG (e.g. each building line with Export to PKG checked) is a different object. The export log shows a warning when the INST file has more than 597 different objects: export fewer elements to PKG, or reuse the same meshes.
- Each terrain patch will become its own block, unless the flag Merge with other connected patches is set (default is true for manually placed patches, false for the ones placed automatically), in that case, when exporting, connected patches will be assigned to the same block. (connected=they share at least one edge)  
Be careful if you add railings, as they can only be placed on the block perimeter.
- Seamless intersections (two roads joined without a surface) don't become blocks. In the BAI file, roads joined this way with the same section and lanes are a single road through the blocks of all of them. Otherwise the joint becomes a two-road intersection using the block of one of the roads, where vehicles never stop. The export log shows a warning (and no BAI road is exported) for roads that end on a seamless intersection that can't be made this way (the other road is not exported, or both blocks are already used by other intersections), and for roads joined only by seamless intersections in a loop.
- If the BAI file is not present, MM2 will place the player's car in the origin point, this can cause the city to be unplayable if there is nothing there. Make sure that there is something at the origin, or alternatively shift everything in Blender (if you're using it) to make the origin contain something.
- Building meshes must be on something that will become a block, else they will not be exported to the .bin file. (as the block for the INST element cannot be determined)
- You can rename building meshes to reorder them, the order will be preserved in the INST file exported. (it can affect rendering through transparent objects)
- Road props will be exported as the prop rules of the game if possible. (propdefs.csv and proprules.csv) This is possible for left and right rules, if the type of the props is **Prop rule**. Other rules (middle, etc., and left and right with another type) will have their props exported as individual props in the PATHSET file.  
**Create ? Props** also contains **Prop line** and **Polygon**. Their full property panel opens before the first point; draw a spline or polygon perimeter using the usual control-point tools. Ctrl+click follows connected anchors. The **Project to ground** toggle updates placement automatically as controls and surfaces move.

- **Prop line** has the type of its props and their fields directly in its panel, and one preset, which saves them too. A new one is a **Pathset line**: as in the game, the model's own X axis lies along each span of the spline, and on each span the spacing (0.25 to 63.75 m in quarter-metre steps) is stretched to fill it, so a span shorter than the spacing gets no props. It is exported as a native path of `props.pathset`, so it has no width. With **Project to ground** on, the props and the path follow the ground, with a point of the path at each prop; with it off, the path is the evaluated spline and the props stay on its straight spans.
- A **Custom line** on a prop line follows the spline with the exact spacing, with model Z along it by default, and each prop is exported on its own, level. **Rotation** turns each prop around the vertical axis by that many degrees from its default direction (0 leaves it along the spline). With **Project to ground** each prop is projected separately, so the ground doesn't change where the props go along the line. On a road a Custom line also has its X position across the road.
- **Max number** set to **0** means **9999** for Custom line, Custom and PSDL prop rule entries, in both the editor and the exported game files. Positive values keep their usual limit.
- With the other props types, the same as those of the road containers, a prop line has a **Width** across the spline, shown with the outer cyan lines. Its evaluated props export individually, including with the **Prop rule** type (except **Parked cars**, below).
- **Parked cars** is a props type for the road containers (such as the parking rows) and for prop lines, for the parked cars that the game places by itself. They are exported to `race/<city>/<city>_parkedcar.pathset` instead of as props. On a prop line they follow the spline, with no width or X position.
  - The game decides which slots of a row are used and which cars they get, with a random paint that the editor doesn't show. The cars and the gaps shown in the editor are only an example. The layout in game stays the same from one launch to the next, but editing the city can change it. To have a specific car in a specific place, use a normal prop.
  - In stock MM2 a slot gets `giz_pcar01_l`, `giz_pcar02_l` or nothing, and the traffic density has no effect. With MM2Hook's default settings it can also get `giz_pcar03_l` (`UseAllParkedCars`), and in Cruise mode the traffic density slider also removes parked cars, with no cars at all when there is no traffic (`DynamicParkedCarDensity`). Turn on **Assume using MM2Hook** in the city properties (Info tab) to have the third model in the example; the exported files are the same either way.
  - **Slot distance** is in metres, from 5.25 to 100. Other values are limited to that range.
  - The cars of a road row follow the traffic of the nearest lanes, so **Left-hand traffic** turns them around. On a prop line they run against the direction of the spline, with either traffic side. **Reverse direction** turns a row around.
  - On a slope the game rolls the cars sideways instead of tilting them along the road, and the editor shows them level. The export warns above 5 degrees: the stock parked cars stay on nearly flat streets.
  - A prop line that belongs to a race goes to `<city>_parkedcar_<race>.pathset`, with the rows of the rest of the city, because in that race the game reads that file instead of the main one.
  - A file can have at most 192 rows. The export reports an error above that and writes the first 192 (with the rows of the race first in a race file). `props.pathset` and the race prop files have the same limit on their paths.
- **Polygon** supports any number of selected models, each with density in props per 100 m? and a yaw range. Its saved seed keeps positions stable. Simple concave perimeters work; self-intersecting or degenerate perimeters show an error. Every generated prop projects separately and exports individually.

All three types support their own property presets, undo/redo, shared or anchored control points, and selection by clicking any generated prop. Migration 5 backs up older cities and removes the experimental standalone prop rules before loading; ordinary road rules and converted road rules are preserved.

## Importing an existing MM2 city

Open an MM2 city and choose **Scripts → Import MM2 city**. Select a loose `city/<name>.psdl`; the source root is detected from its folder, or can be supplied explicitly. The importer looks for BAI, INST, prop definitions/rules, prop/decal pathsets, train paths, CINFO, AIMAP and race files in that root. Extract archives separately. No original game data is included in the core.

The import appends to the open city. Rooms, attributes and AI become editable custom elements, sharing source vertices and matching existing anchors. INST objects and point props become mesh instances. Source model/texture folders are added to local asset paths, without copying assets. Missing assets use the editor's defaults and are reported. Keep the source folders available after moving the builder city.

Single-room prop rules stay attached to road custom elements. Rules spanning rooms become standalone **Prop rules**, with editable boundary ribbons and road-center distance references. Spacing and count limits continue across their segments. Native `props.pathset` line strips also stay procedural. Export writes native type-2 strips when their evaluated placement exactly matches the format's spacing/orientation semantics (1 cm position, 0.1° yaw tolerance); otherwise it writes individual props. Independent strips sharing a model remain separate.

Source city settings and traffic-model lists are applied by default; turn off **Apply source city settings** to retain the current ones. Races, decals and train lines append. Repeating the import deliberately appends again. City edits form one undo step and roll back on failure/cancellation. Asset search paths are project preferences outside undo; cancellation can leave these paths registered.

Import/re-export is a semantic conversion, not a binary-preserving round trip. BAI lane samples, permissions and connections use the existing editable types, but multi-room route membership is reduced to one owner, arbitrary BAI train splines are omitted, and distances, frames, sidewalk centers, lights and culling are regenerated. Symmetric tram rails use measured offsets. Curved pedestrian paths can differ materially. The console reports these approximations, unsupported flags/tuning, missing companions and missing assets. Source random prop choices can differ. Decals with variable widths and sheared INST transforms are approximated; maps are regenerated. Native PKG/TEX/BND/MTX and tuning assets are referenced rather than reconstructed.

Developer checks: `python scripts/importCity/_test_import_city.py` runs short synthetic parser/mapping/strip tests. `_test_builder.py` runs through the builder's script CLI with `KM2_IMPORT_TEST_FOLDER` naming a new scratch folder; it covers import, generic ribbon sampling, undo/redo, save/reload, settings, INST transforms and native strip re-export. `_roundtrip_check.py source/city/name.psdl export/city/name.psdl --sample 10 --seed 42` writes a JSON semantic audit to stdout; `--out` saves it and `--strict` fails on compared differences. `_test_stock.py` is an optional bounded check of five routes per stock city with `KM2_MM2_SOURCE_ROOT` pointing at the extracted game root. Use writable `TMP`/`TEMP` where needed. None of these tests requires committing stock assets.

`_test_sidewalks.py` uses the same builder CLI and environment variables for a short sidewalk regression: synthetic strips and end caps, plus ten fixed-seed stock attributes when a source root is supplied. It checks the generated mesh, point order, cap markers, undo/redo, save/reload and PSDL re-export.

The fixed-seed ten-route SF/London check preserves sampled lane counts and ambient permissions, with lane-position differences below 0.03 mm. Existing exporter vertex welding changes some surface vertices by up to 3.3 cm. On two curved London routes, sidewalk curves stay close but their samples at corresponding road sections shift by 10–14 m as sections are regenerated. Close lanes alone therefore do not establish equivalent pedestrian or traffic behavior. This bounded check does not replace an in-game check of a whole imported city.

### Decal migration

Core version 3 migrates manual decals from city properties into spline-driven custom elements, preserving their names, textures, widths and centerline points as low-poly splines. The normal migration creates backups first and also preserves incomplete decals for editing. The old Decals tab and automatic rail settings are removed. **Previously automatic rail decals are not baked:** generate intersection connections with the new button and place any road-length decals separately.

## Assume using MM2Hook

By default the core assumes stock MM2. Turn on **Assume using MM2Hook** in the city properties (Info tab) if the city is meant to be played with MM2Hook with its default settings:

- The **Roll** of the road points can be edited. It stays disabled otherwise, but a roll already set still applies and is exported, so a banked road keeps its banking until you turn this on and change it.
- The example parked cars include the third model (see **Parked cars**).
- The export warnings about more than 511 texture references describe the limits with MM2Hook instead of those of stock MM2.

## Direction arrows in the editor

Vehicle and cable-car lane lines show repeated arrows when the lines are visible, including **View > Show all traffic lanes**. The arrows follow the city's **Drive on left** setting, on both procedural roads and custom BAI roads. Pedestrian lanes and alleys stay plain because pedestrians travel both ways.

Moving-object loops (boats, ducks, tugs and ferries) and opponent waypoint lines also show arrows in path order. Train lines stay plain because trains run back and forth. Arrows are editor overlays and do not change the exported paths.
